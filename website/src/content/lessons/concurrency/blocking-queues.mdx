## A producer and consumer can run at different rates

A producer discovers work while a consumer processes it. A bounded queue lets a short burst wait in storage. When full, the insertion API decides whether to wait, fail immediately or wait only within a budget. When empty, the consumer makes the corresponding retrieval choice.

A BlockingQueue combines ordering/storage with those waiting operations. Blocking describes caller behavior; not every implementation supplies a useful capacity bound. Null elements are prohibited because retrieval APIs use null for absence or timeout.

The consumer knows this particular batch contains exactly three items. Join establishes completed result observation, and finally accounts for the owned worker if the producer is interrupted. This is a finite batch protocol, not a generic rule that an empty queue means producers finished. Removing an item also does not acknowledge successful business processing or create durable/exactly-once delivery.

## Choose the method's ordinary failure policy

| Operation | Throw on ordinary failure | Special result | Wait | Bounded wait |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insert | add | offer | put | timed offer |
| Remove | remove | poll | take | timed poll |
| Inspect | element | peek | Not supplied | Not supplied |

A false offer means no item was accepted. Interrupted waits report interruption rather than invent a successful transfer. A deadline for the parent operation may include queue admission and processing, not just one call to offer.

## Trace FIFO and closure

BlockingQueue has no universal close method. Applications can coordinate known producer completion, interruption or sentinel messages. A poison-pill design needs sufficient terminal signals for its consumers and a rule excluding late producers. Null cannot be its sentinel.

For an implementation exercise, make lifecycle part of the guarded state. This queue defines close-and-drain: reject new puts, preserve accepted elements, and return null only after closed and empty.

Head, tail, size and closed share one lock. Constructor validation establishes positive capacity. Put rejects null, acquires interruptibly, waits while full/open and rechecks closure before mutation. Take waits while empty/open, clears a delivered slot and signals capacity. Close sets the flag and signals all on both conditions. Existing elements remain drainable.

## Make termination work for every consumer

The finite three-item example has one consumer and an exact batch size. For two consumers, sending one poison pill is insufficient: one can receive it and exit while the other waits forever. A sentinel protocol needs one terminal receipt per consumer, no late producers after termination signals, and a failure policy if a producer exits before publishing those signals.

Do not place an unconditional blocking put of a terminal signal in an interrupted producer's finally and assume cleanup cannot fail. Interruption, full capacity or a failed consumer can prevent that put. A close-and-drain queue instead changes guarded lifecycle state and wakes both waiter populations; accepted items remain distinguishable from the closed-and-empty condition.

**Failure exercise:** a consumer removes an item and then fails while processing it. The queue has already relinquished that element. Neither take nor producer completion automatically retries the business action. Decide acknowledgement/retry/idempotency separately from FIFO storage and permit/resource accounting. A bounded queue is an in-process coordination mechanism, not durable delivery. [BlockingQueue usage and publication](https://docs.oracle.com/javase/8/docs/api/java/util/concurrent/BlockingQueue.html)

## Queue representation and alternatives

ArrayBlockingQueue uses a fixed array, count, put/take indexes, one ReentrantLock and separate notEmpty/notFull Conditions. The complete state transition occurs under that lock; an awakened thread reacquires and loops. Linearization is the protected insertion/removal transition, not the earlier signal that a thread started.

LinkedBlockingQueue uses linked nodes, distinct put/take locks and count/signalling coordination; give it a useful explicit capacity. SynchronousQueue performs a rendezvous without buffered storage. PriorityBlockingQueue orders retrieval but is logically unbounded; DelayQueue adds time eligibility. These are distinct admission/order policies, not equivalent backpressure switches.

Actions before inserting an object are published to another thread following its documented access/removal. Later unsynchronized mutation of that object remains a separate sharing problem. Fair lock selection influences acquisition, not every end-to-end completion time.

## Exercise and complete reference solution

Implement put, take and close-and-drain with wrapping storage and interruptible waits. `CloseableWorkQueue` supplies the complete solution. Explain why close uses signalAll on both Conditions and why take clears a slot. The source intentionally omits full BlockingQueue interface support, iterators, fairness and timed methods.

For a timed extension, carry one remaining nanosecond budget through awaitNanos, recheck full/open in a loop, and preserve the chosen closed-state failure policy. Regranting a full timeout at each wakeup breaks the overall waiting budget.
