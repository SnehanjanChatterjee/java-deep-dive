## Workers must finish this round before the next one

A **CyclicBarrier** is a reusable meeting point for a fixed number of participants, called **parties**. Each party calls await when it reaches the end of its current round. The early arrivals wait until the last required arrival, after which the barrier can be used for another round. One use of the meeting point is a **generation**.

This fits a calculation where two workers repeatedly produce separate partial values. Each round's partials must be combined before either worker overwrites its slot for the next round. A one-use latch would remain open after round one; the barrier keeps those repeated rounds separate.

A **barrier action** is an optional Runnable executed by the last arriving party before releasing that generation's waiters. Here it combines the two result slots. The ordinary sequence is therefore produce partial, arrive and wait, combine when both are ready, then proceed to the next round. First trace two successful rounds; the later example shows what happens when a required arrival fails.

## Run two complete generations

<JavaExample file="BarrierLesson.java" output={"phase total=3\nphase total=6"} />

In round one, the two slots become one and two. Either worker may arrive first, but the action cannot run until both arrive. It reads their sum three. Only after the action completes do the waits return. In round two, each worker updates its own slot to two or four, giving six.

The barrier action is part of the correctness of this program: it reads the slots before released workers may begin overwriting them. Moving that sum into one worker after await would let the other worker start the next round first. A reusable rendezvous is not a guarantee that all statements after a barrier happen together.

## Observe a broken generation

A generation is **broken** when its normal all-parties-arrived completion can no longer be trusted, such as after interruption of a waiting participant. Peers leave abnormally rather than treating partial work as a completed round. This experiment creates that failure deliberately and distinguishes the interrupted participant from its peer.

<JavaExample file="BarrierFailureLesson.java" output={"interrupted status=false\nbroken=true\npeer saw broken generation"} />

The first worker interrupts itself before await. Await detects that pending request, clears its status while throwing InterruptedException, and breaks this generation. Main joins the first worker before arriving as the second participant, so it deterministically observes the broken generation through BrokenBarrierException. This experiment needs no delay intended to make one participant arrive first.

The distinction matters: the interrupted participant has an interruption outcome; a peer's broken-generation exception does not mean that peer was itself interrupted. A timed-out participant similarly invalidates that generation rather than quietly leaving everyone else waiting for a missing arrival.

If the barrier action itself fails, the last arriver executing it sees that failure and the generation breaks for peers. Reset starts a fresh generation, but does not repair their application state or establish which old participants should retry. Decide recovery as a group before reusing the synchronization point. [CyclicBarrier breakage and action contract](https://docs.oracle.com/javase/8/docs/api/java/util/concurrent/CyclicBarrier.html)

## Failure breaks the current generation

A worker interrupted while waiting or a timed wait that expires can break the generation, causing other waiting parties to receive BrokenBarrierException. A barrier action that throws also breaks the generation. Catching that exception and treating the phase as successful would use incomplete/inconsistent partials.

The source records each worker's failure and the owner checks those fields after join. Finally interrupts and joins owned workers if the owner exits early. A task group also needs enough available execution capacity to admit every party. Two blocked pool workers cannot reach a three-party barrier when its third task remains queued.

Reset starts a different generation and breaks existing waits. It does not repair application state or coordinate which logical iteration participants should retry. Cancelling/recreating an entire failed computation is often clearer than letting unrelated retries mix generations.
