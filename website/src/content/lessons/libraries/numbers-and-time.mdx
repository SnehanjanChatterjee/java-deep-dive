## Calculate an invoice from decimal inputs

Three items cost 19.95 each and tax is 8.25 percent. The intended quantities are decimal: subtotal 59.85, tax 4.937625 before rounding, tax 4.94 at the invoice boundary, total 64.79. BigDecimal lets the program express those quantities and choose its rounding policy explicitly.

BigDecimal is immutable. add, subtract, multiply and divide return results; they do not change an operand. Ignoring amount.add(fee) leaves amount unchanged. A final reference alone would not supply this property; it comes from the value type.

The constructor receives "19.95" as a String, preserving the declared decimal spelling. BigDecimal.valueOf(3) represents the quantity. Multiplication computes the exact subtotal. The tax rate 0.0825 is a proportion, so 8.25 percent must not be entered as 8.25.

total requires a nonnegative unit price, positive quantity and nonnegative tax rate. It accepts prices that fit two fractional places exactly, then rounds tax once per invoice with HALF_EVEN. It is one declared currency/minor-unit domain; two places are an example policy, not a universal rule for money.

<span id="mechanics-and-tradeoffs" aria-hidden="true"></span>
## Distinguish value, representation and rounding

A BigDecimal stores an unscaled integer and a scale. For 19.95, those are 1995 and 2: 1995 × 10^-2. Scale is not precision. Precision counts significant digits; scale locates the decimal point and may be negative.

Constructing from a double records information already present in that binary floating-point value. new BigDecimal(0.1) does not recover a user's original decimal spelling. valueOf(double) uses its canonical decimal string representation, but also cannot restore information lost in an earlier calculation. Use a String or validated integer minor units when those are the actual input contract.

setScale(2, UNNECESSARY) asserts the unit price needs no rounding to fit the domain. setScale(2, HALF_EVEN) on computed tax allows rounding under a specific tie policy. These are different operations. The dedicated BigDecimal chapter examines precision contexts, ties, division and exact conversion in detail.

## Keep units and boundaries in the model

A fixed-minor-unit long can be simpler when every amount has the same declared scale and exact arithmetic checks overflow. BigDecimal is useful when decimal arithmetic/rounding is itself part of the domain. Binary floating point remains appropriate for approximate scientific quantities. Choose based on meaning, not the slogan that one numeric type is always correct.

Per-item tax rounding and per-invoice rounding can produce different totals. The method's named boundary makes that choice reviewable. Adding amounts in different currencies requires another value model, not merely a wider numeric type.

## Separate arithmetic from presentation

Formatting a value with two printed places does not establish the intermediate calculation policy. BigDecimal.equals includes representation scale: 1.0 and 1.00 are unequal, while compareTo returns zero. The next chapter develops the collection/equality consequences. Date and time use different concepts and are taught in their own fundamentals and zone-boundary chapters.

## Completed exercise: zero tax and invalid precision

Call total(new BigDecimal("19.95"), 3, BigDecimal.ZERO): the subtotal is 59.85 and tax is 0.00, so the result is 59.85. Call it with "1.001": UNNECESSARY rejects the unit price rather than quietly rounding it. A different application can choose to round input prices, but that would be a different public promise.

## Work out two different rounding boundaries

For three items priced 19.95 at the example rate, the boundary changes the result even with the same RoundingMode:

| Calculation | Per item, then sum | Sum first, then invoice tax |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact price × rate | 1.645875 each | 4.937625 on subtotal 59.85 |
| Rounded tax | 1.65 each | 4.94 once |
| Combined tax | 4.95 | 4.94 |
| Total including subtotal | 64.80 | 64.79 |

Both are internally consistent policies. The supplied total method implements the second one; moving setScale inside a per-item loop changes that promise. In the complete arithmetic program in the next lesson, item tax and invoice tax are printed separately so the difference can be reproduced.

For discounts or allocations, ask whether every component must be rounded independently and how any remainder is assigned. Formatting two decimal places afterward cannot repair a total computed under another rounding rule. Keep intermediate arithmetic, domain rounding and display formatting as explicit separate stages.
